# Abstract

Thumbnails and titles are the first cues users see on YouTube, yet little is known about how their visual and linguistic style relates to audience age in ordinary information content. We analyze 9,586 Korean YouTube videos from four categories with balanced age consumption (EDU, HEALTH, LIFESTYLE, and SOCIETY), drawn from channels whose primary audience is aged 34 and under (~34) or 65 and over (65+). A thumbnail-only classifier and Grad-CAM first locate where the age signal is carried. Those regions are then measured as visual features, as scene topics from vision-language captions, and as formal and lexical properties of titles. Large text area, strong text–background contrast, longer titles, list-enumeration patterns, and native Korean vocabulary are associated with 65+ content in all four categories, while emoji, brackets, and loanwords are associated with ~34 content. Semantic scenes follow category context: self-presentation versus health information in HEALTH, nature and healing versus religious symbols in LIFESTYLE, and active scenes versus interpersonal conflict in SOCIETY. Age-related style cannot be read from one category's conventions alone; the features that recur across the four categories and the features that change with each category have to be taken together.
