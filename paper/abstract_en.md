# Abstract

Older and younger audiences share the same video platform, but the thumbnails and titles that introduce content to them may not look or read alike. We ask whether presentation style is associated with audience age, and whether any such difference holds across content domains. We analyze 9,586 Korean YouTube videos from four categories selected for balanced age consumption (education, health, lifestyle, and society), drawn from channels whose primary audience is aged 34 and under or 65 and over. Rather than fixing a feature list in advance, we train a thumbnail-only classifier and use Grad-CAM to locate the regions that carry the age signal, then measure those regions as visual features, as scene topics from vision-language captions, and as lexical and grammatical properties of titles. Two kinds of signals emerge. Text area, text–background contrast, longer titles, list-enumeration patterns, and native Korean vocabulary are associated with older-audience content in all four categories, while emoji, brackets, and loanwords are associated with younger-audience content. Semantic scene content, in contrast, is category-specific: mirror selfies in health, Buddhist symbols in meditation, and conflictual interpersonal scenes in society. Age-related style therefore combines presentational conventions that recur across categories with imagery that remains genre-specific, rather than following a single grammar. These are associations in how content is presented, prior to recommendation or exposure.
