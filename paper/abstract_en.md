# Abstract

Thumbnails and titles are the first cues users see on YouTube, yet little is known about how their visual and linguistic style relates to audience age in ordinary information content. We analyze 9,586 Korean YouTube videos from four categories with balanced age consumption (EDU, HEALTH, LIFESTYLE, and SOCIETY), drawn from channels whose primary audience is aged 34 and under (~34) or 65 and over (65+). A thumbnail-only classifier and Grad-CAM first locate where the age signal is carried. Those regions are then measured as visual features, as scene topics from vision-language captions, and as formal and lexical properties of titles. Two kinds of signals emerge. Large text area, strong text–background contrast, longer titles, list-enumeration patterns, and native Korean vocabulary are associated with 65+ content in all four categories, while emoji, brackets, and loanwords are associated with ~34 content. Semantic scenes, by contrast, vary by category: self-presentation versus health information in HEALTH, nature and healing versus religious symbols in LIFESTYLE, and active scenes versus interpersonal conflict in SOCIETY. Age-related style therefore has both shared features that run in the same direction across the four categories, and features that change with the context of each category.
