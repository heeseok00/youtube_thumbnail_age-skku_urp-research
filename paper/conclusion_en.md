# 4. Conclusion

This study analyzed multimodal differences in thumbnails and titles associated with the 34-and-under and 65-and-over groups in four categories of YouTube content: EDU, HEALTH, LIFESTYLE, and SOCIETY. Content associated with the 65-and-over group showed, overall, a higher text ratio and stronger text–background contrast. Its titles were also relatively longer and drew more on vocabulary of life circumstances, relationships, illness, religion, and self-cultivation. Content associated with the 34-and-under group used a more restrained visual composition and relied more on loanwords, emoji, and expressions centered on content format and activity.

These differences did not take the same form in every category. Semantic scenes of people, actions, expressions, and settings varied with content context: self-presentation versus health information in HEALTH, nature and healing versus religious symbols in LIFESTYLE, and active scenes versus conflictual interpersonal scenes in SOCIETY. Age-related multimodal style therefore combines shared forms of presentation across categories with genre-specific semantic context, rather than following a single fixed grammar.

The results suggest that audience age is visible in recurring visual and linguistic conventions in thumbnails and titles, rather than remaining only a metadata label on channels. Age-aware content design should attend not only to readability and information access, but also to conventions that repeatedly tie a given age group to a restricted set of life situations and images. By presenting both the cross-category consistency and the category specificity of these age-related expressions, this study provides a basis for later work on age-related information accessibility, the presentation of age in digital content, and visual and textual content design.
